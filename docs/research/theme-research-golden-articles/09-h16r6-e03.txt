U.S. gas-fired power capacity planned or under development more than tripled in 2025 to 252 gigawatts (GW), according to Global Energy Monitor. U.S. gas turbine manufacturing capacity is forecast to rise by 15 GW over the next 12 months - up from current estimates of around 20 to 30 GW - and capacity could reach 70 to 80 GW by 2030. Some of this capacity is expected to supply markets outside the U.S. and gas plant developers could also be limited by component bottlenecks and technical labor shortages.

The U.S. order pipeline for gas turbines soared from 7 GW in 2020 to 44 GW in 2025, according to Wood Mackenzie estimates. Lead times for large gas turbines are exceeding five years. Lead times for small gas turbines are currently 18 to 36 months, but those seem to be getting longer as well.

NRG has reserved 2.4 GW of planned capacity for turbines powered by GE Vernova, expected to come online in 2029 and 2030. Long lead times have increased demand for gas-engine-based generation. Wärtsilä has received three large U.S. engine orders since July 2025 totaling 1.2 GW, with deliveries planned to start in late 2026 and continue into 2027.
