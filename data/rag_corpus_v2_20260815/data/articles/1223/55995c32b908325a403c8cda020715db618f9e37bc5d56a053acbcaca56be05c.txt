Its main lineup includes 300 Mbps, 500 Mbps, and 1 Gig plans, with even faster multi-gig fiber options available in some areas. The right Optimum internet plan largely depends on how many people and devices are sharing the connection. The 300 Mbps tier is enough for common activities like browsing, video calls, and streaming across several devices, while the 500 Mbps tier offers more headroom for households that stream 4K video on multiple screens or use many connected devices at once. The 1 Gig plan is geared toward heavier usage, such as gaming, frequent large downloads/uploads, and households with multiple people streaming or working online simultaneously. It's important to check what type of Optimum service is available at your address. Optimum operates both a fiber-to-the-home network and a hybrid fiber-coaxial cable network, so upload speeds can differ substantially, even when the advertised download tier is the same. For example, Optimum lists cable versions of its 300 Mbps and 500 Mbps plans with upload speeds up to 20 Mbps, and its 1 Gig cable plan with download speeds up to 940 Mbps and upload speeds up to 35 Mbps. Fiber customers can get symmetrical speeds, including multi-gig service in eligible markets. Optimum also includes equipment and setup benefits, though some costs can rise over time. Current offers include free self-setup and a free Gateway for the first 12 months; after that, the Gateway fee can increase by no more than $5 a month every year until the standard rate is reached. Optional Whole Home WiFi costs $5 a month, and Optimum may provide an extender when needed. Shoppers can also bundle internet, TV, and mobile services for additional savings of up to $30 a month.
